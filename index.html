HOW IT WORKS

Co-op campaign: everyone types the same room code. The first one in hosts: it is the host's campaign (from the CAMPAIGN tab) that everyone flies in, and the host's game runs the war, so keep it open. Friends join on the blue side, take off from a blue airfield and fight the red air force and ground forces together. Pick your jet on the HANGAR tab and your stores on the CAMPAIGN tab before joining. Shot down? Press Enter to go again. Land at a friendly field to rearm.

Dogfight: everyone fights everyone; shot-down pilots respawn.

Players connect directly over the internet (WebRTC); no account is needed. Works from the standalone file too.

SITUATION

Fly either side from a 3D cockpit. Fuel burns, engines spool, drop tanks feed first, heavy stores cost turn rate, and sustained G greys you out and can knock you out. Radars scan bar by bar and lose beaming targets in the notch; ridges block them outright. Semi-active missiles need your lock to impact; heat-seekers see a burner from much further away. Missiles fly real motors and airframes: they burn propellant, boost then sustain, and bleed speed in every hard turn, so a late break or a long shot low down defeats them. Chaff only fools a radar seeker that has the target in its notch, and only some of the time. Radar detection is statistical: contacts flicker in at the edge of range and TWS tracks drift when the target manoeuvres.

AIRCRAFT NOTES

ECM. The F-16 carries an ALQ-131 pod (ALQ-184 in the modern era), the F-15C an internal ALQ-135 and the Su-27 L-005S pods on its wingtip rails (clear stations 1 and 10 to fit them); the MiG-29 and MiG-23 have no jammer. NOISE hides you from a radar beyond its burn-through range (it only sees a bearing strobe, and a HOJ lock gives bearing but no range), DECM walks a tracking radar's range gate off you and breaks its lock or its missile's, and AUTO transmits deception only while you are tracked. A noise jammer pauses every few seconds to look through. Radar missiles without a home-on-jam seeker (R-27T/ET aside, the R-24 and older AIM-7s) cannot track through noise until they burn through; AIM-7M, AIM-120C, R-77 and the R-27R / ER can. The ground radars obey the same rules: the SA-6 and the Shilka's Gun Dish lose you inside your noise until burn-through. Heat-seekers and the IRST ignore ECM.

F/A-18C. Lot 20 Hornet: two F404-GE-402s, a +7.5 g / 35° AoA digital limiter (the limiter is the ceiling here, so you cannot depart it by pulling), the APG-73 with 5 / 10 / 20 / 40 / 80 / 160 NM scales, an internal ALQ-126B (ALQ-165 ASPJ in the modern era) and the ALE-39 / ALE-47 dispensers. Stations 1 / 9 take Sidewinders only, 2 / 8 and 3 / 7 any missile (3, 5 and 7 carry the 330 gal tanks), 4 / 6 are the semi-recessed Sparrow / AMRAAM stations. In the cockpit the left DDI starts on SMS, the right DDI on the attack radar and the AMPCD on the HSI; every display has a MENU button (OSB 18, left edge, middle) that lists RDR, SMS, HSI, FCS, ENG, FUEL and EW, and the button bezel around each display is clickable. On the radar page OSB 1 cycles RWS / TWS / ACM, 2 and 3 set azimuth and bars, 4 is OPR / STBY, 7 is CNCL and 19 / 20 change range; click the scope to place the cursor and lock. The SMS page selects the weapon on the top row and OSB 6 is MASTER ARM; S-J jettisons tanks. The stick has the castle (TDC / lock), weapon select and undesignate; the throttle has the countermeasures dispenser; the IFEI UP / DWN buttons set BINGO. Approach AoA bracket in the HUD and the indexer left of it come on with the gear down (on speed 8.1°).

F-4E. Conventional hydromechanical controls (no fly-by-wire, no limiter): it will depart if you hold high AoA with rudder, and the J79s smoke below about 90% RPM. The APQ-120 is a pulse radar: no TWS, ground clutter in look-down, and AIM-7s need a lock (STT). Cold-war loads: AIM-9B/D/E/G/J and AIM-7E/E-2/F; AIM-9P/L and AIM-7P are modern only. Missile figures are approximations of public data.

F-14A. Early Tomcat with the AN/ALR-23 IRST pod under the nose. The wings follow the CADC sweep program (20° slow, 68° from Mach 1) on their own; . sweeps them further aft by hand and , brings them back to AUTO. The TF30s are slow to spool, there is no AoA limiter, and with spoilers for roll it yaws the wrong way at high AoA, so roll with rudder there. The pilot has no radar scope: the HSD repeats the RIO's TID (unknown = half box, hostile = hat, Phoenix firing order numbers, then time to impact) and the VDI and HUD show the attack picture. Your AI RIO runs the AWG-9: he works the range scale and antenna elevation, builds TWS track files and holds the Phoenix priority, goes STT for Sparrows and inside 10 miles, takes jammers home-on-jam, and calls contacts, locks, shots, spikes and missiles on the intercom. Your radar keys become requests to him: Enter lock (the contact nearest your nose), Tab next priority, U drop, 4 TWS / RWS; T is the pilot's ACM (PAL → VSL HI → VSL LO → PLM). 6 takes the radar yourself. In TWS each Space sends a Phoenix to the next track in firing order (up to six in the air), guided by the track files until it goes active about 10 NM out. AIM-54C is modern only.

MiG-21bis. A light delta-wing point-defence fighter: one R-25-300, 23 m² wing, hydraulic controls with no limiter, so it will pitch up and depart if you hold the stick back at low speed, and it bleeds energy fast in a turn. Its RP-22SM is a short-range pulse radar (about 20 km, ground clutter in look-down, a lock is the only way to guide a missile) seen on the round scope in the middle of the panel. Weapons: R-13M and R-3S infra-red missiles (rear-aspect, narrow seeker), R-60M on the modern loadout, and the GSh-23L with 200 rounds. Fuel is tight: hang the PTB-800 on the centreline, or drop the tanks with SEL JETT before you fight. The SPO-10 warner shows which side a radar is on, nothing more.

MiG-25PD. A Mach 2.83 interceptor built around two R-15BD-300s: it accelerates to M2.8 at altitude and holds it, but the limit is real. The airframe-temperature lamp (T 290°) lights at the stagnation temperature of M2.83, past it the engines overheat and the thrust falls away and then burns the engines, and at low level it is held to about 1,250 km/h IAS. It is a straight-line fighter: +5 g clean (3.8 g with the belly tank, the structure goes near 6.7 g), a low roll rate and a soft AoA limit near 17° with a flat spin behind it. The Sapfir-25 pulse radar (about 85 km detection and 60 km tracking against a fighter, look-down about 30% shorter, MTI trades range for clutter rejection) is shown on the hooded scope in the middle of the panel; the PKI collimator ring is the sight, the TP-26Sh1 IRST is the passive mode and the SPO-10 is the only warner. No gun and no countermeasures. Weapons: R-40RD (radar) and R-40TD (infra-red), huge and slow to turn but effective at 50 km from a high, fast launch, and a pair of R-60M on each outer pylon instead of an R-40. The belly tank is the PTB-5300; jettison it with SEL JETT before manoeuvring.

NOTES. US jets read knots and feet, Russian jets km/h and metres. Neither side gets a missile approach warner: the RWR only calls radar missiles while they are guided at you, and heat-seekers come silent. The IRST on the MiG-29 and Su-27 tracks without setting off your RWR. The F-15C has no hard AoA limiter; the Su-27 departs late but still departs. The MiG-23ML is a swing-wing jet with conventional controls: , / . move the sweep handle (16° slow and turning, 45° fighting, 72° fast; overspeed with the wing forward breaks it). Its Sapfir-23ML is a pulse radar: the picture on the sight glass is raw video full of ground clutter, look-down is weak, and the MTI (MTI) switch trades range for a cleaner screen.

DATALINK. The MiG-29, Su-27, F-14A, F-15C and F/A-18C pool their radars across the flight: a bandit that only a wingman has painted shows as a dashed diamond (amber on the F/A-18C), tagged with the number of the wingman reporting it, and the flight is drawn as circles (arches on the F-14 TID). A link track is a few seconds old and fades after 7 s; a jet that is not fitted gets nothing. Su-27: the usual scope, with link tracks and the flight added. MiG-29: the PAGE switch under the HDD flips between the HUD repeater and the Su-27 scope with the datalink picture. F-14A: the DATA LINK button on the TID panel. F-15C: the MPCD (lower left, the PACS display) has ARMT, HSD and HSI pages on the bottom row of its pushbuttons; the HSD is the tactical datalink picture (top row: north up / heading up, link tracks; right side: range). Link tracks also show on the VSD. F/A-18C: the new SA page (MENU) and a LINK key on the HSI page; link tracks also show on the radar page. Click a link contact (SA page or the amber diamond on the radar page), or press OSB 4 on the SA page to step through them, to designate it; with an AIM-120 selected and no radar lock, the trigger fires at it (DLNK on the HUD) and the missile is steered by the link picture until its seeker goes active.

TWS (MiG-29 / Su-27). Press 4 for track-while-scan (HUD column A2 / TWS, head-down display TWS n/10). The N019 and N001 keep up to ten track files while the beam keeps searching, and the HUD scope and head-down display draw them extrapolated between sweeps. The most dangerous closing track is bugged for you (dashed box) until you take over with Enter on the cursor or Tab; Enter again on the bug goes to STT and U un-bugs. An R-77 leaves from the bug with no lock; an R-27R or R-27ER puts the radar into STT as you pull the trigger, and the set locks on by itself at 85 % of the missile's maximum range. A jammer strobe on the scope throws the radar back to normal search. The MiG-21, MiG-23 and MiG-25 pulse radars have no TWS.

NCTR. The F/A-18C's OSB 5 on the radar page. With a lock (or a TWS bug, or the track under the cursor) and the target nose-on or tail-on inside about 70 % of the radar's detection range, the percentage climbs for a few seconds and then the type is printed beside the track (and passed over the link). Beam aspect, jamming, the look-down notch and range stop it.
